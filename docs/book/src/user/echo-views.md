# Standard views: find the view with the probe

The **Views** section of the Echo page's left column (its *Standard views* card) asks you for one of the standard transthoracic views (the ones taught in the
American Society of Echocardiography guideline) and scores your probe against it. The score uses the model's geometry only.
It says how close you are, gives one hint, and ticks off what the view has to show. The targets, the tolerances and the hint
wording are **designed** for this model. No sonographer or clinician has reviewed them.

The left column has four sections now: **Probe**, **Image**, **Views** and **Measure**. The measuring tools (M-mode, tissue Doppler and calipers) are on [their own page](echo-measuring.md).

> **For education only.** This teaches orientation and view recognition in this model. It does not teach hand skill on a
> patient, and a good score is not a clinical result.

![The parasternal long axis, found, with its checklist](../images/echo-view-plax.png)

## Practise and self-test

The section has two modes, *Practise* and *Self-test*, chosen with the control at its top.

**Practise.** Pick a view from the list, grouped by window. The card shows:

* a **score bar** from 0 to 100 %;
* one **hint** in words, for the largest correction to make first (the hints are listed below);
* a **checklist** of what the view must show, updated live from the image: a tick in a green circle for each line the image
  meets, and an empty ring for each line it does not;
* **Show me**: moves the probe to the reference pose of the view. It animates over 1.4 s, and it is instant with
  Reduced motion on;
* a **ghost** of the target plane in the 3-D heart, which you can switch off;
* **Name structures on the image**: names the structures of the current view on the image, which you can switch off.

A view is **found** when the score stays at 80 % or more for one second. The page then says *Found: the view has been held
for a second*.

![The apical four-chamber view, with the ghost, the labels and the checklist](../images/echo-view-a4c.png)

**Self-test.** The self-test asks for the shipped views one at a time, in the order of the list. It gives:

* no hints, no checklist, no ghost and no labels;
* a clock for each view, which runs until the view is found or until you choose **Give up on this view**;
* at the end, a summary table with the best score and the best time for each view, and the mean of the best scores. The
  **Time** column shows "—" for a view that was not found; the **Best** column still shows the highest score reached.

There is **no pass mark**. The self-test shows your scores and times only. Your progress is saved like the other quizzes.

When you start the self-test, the page first prepares the reference pose of each view, one view per frame, so the page stays
responsive while it works. A progress bar reads *Preparing views n of 15* (the list has fifteen views, the two that cannot be
reached included). Each reference pose is searched once, the first time it is needed. The total time has not been measured
since the page was changed to prepare one view per frame.

![The self-test: scripted screenshot](../images/echo-selftest.png)

*The self-test screenshot is scripted: the probe, the scores and the times were set by a test script to show the layout.
They are not real results, and the times are not a measurement of anyone.*

## The views

Thirteen views are shipped. Each section gives where the probe goes, what the view must show, and the usual error. The
guideline page is in brackets. The marker rule is either given in the guideline or **designed**; the section says which.

### Parasternal long axis

*Mitchell et al. 2019, pages 23 to 25.* Put the probe in the parasternal window, beside the left sternal border, at the third
or fourth intercostal space. The marker points toward the right shoulder, at about 9 to 10 o'clock.

You should see the left ventricle, the left atrium and the aortic root, the mitral leaflets and the aortic cusps. No papillary
muscle should be in the plane. The apex must be **outside** the sector.

*Common mistake:* the apex is in the image. A short, rounded ventricle with no apex is a false apex: rotate, tilt or angle the
beam to fix it.

### Right ventricular outflow

*Page 25.* From the long axis, tilt the beam anteriorly and rotate slightly clockwise. The marker rule is **designed**: the
marker points from the right ventricle toward the pulmonary artery.

You should see the right ventricle, the pulmonary artery and the pulmonary valve cusps.

*Common mistake:* a rib or the lung covers part of the sector. At the reference pose this view has the largest hidden share of
all the views (half of the sector), so expect shadows; the hint names the rib when one is in the way.

### Right ventricular inflow

*Page 25.* From the long axis, tilt the probe toward the patient's right hip, with a little counterclockwise rotation if
needed. The marker rule is **designed**: the guideline gives no angle for it.

You should see the right atrium in the lower part of the image, the right ventricle in the upper part, and the tricuspid
leaflets in the centre.

*Common mistake:* the marker is on the wrong side, or the tricuspid valve is not in the centre. The page names a wrong marker
side when the marker is more than 110° from its target.

### Parasternal short axis: aortic valve

*Page 25.* From the long axis, rotate the probe 90° clockwise, so the beam cuts across the ventricle. Tilt toward the base of
the heart; one interspace higher often helps. The marker points toward the patient's left shoulder; the guideline gives this
as the 90° turn, and the marker rule is **designed**.

You should see the aortic cusps in the centre, the left atrium behind the aorta, and the right ventricle in front.

*Common mistake:* the plane still runs along the ventricle, so the left ventricle looks like a long shape rather than a cut
section. The score penalises a plane that is too oblique to the long axis.

### Parasternal short axis: mitral valve

*Page 26.* From the aortic-valve level, tilt toward the apex, inferiorly and slightly leftward, and stop at the mitral valve.
The marker rule is **designed**, as for the aortic-valve view.

You should see the mitral leaflets, the left ventricle cut across with its wall around it, and the right ventricle as a
crescent at the top and left of the sector. The checklist asks for the leaflets, the blood of the left ventricle, its wall and
the right ventricle.

*Common mistake:* the left ventricle looks oval rather than round. The score asks for a cut that is round enough (a ratio of
0.85 or more between its short and long axes).

### Parasternal short axis: papillary muscles

*Page 26.* Tilt a little further, to just below the tips of the mitral leaflets. The marker rule is **designed**.

You should see the left ventricle as a circle with its wall cut evenly all round, and both papillary muscles as round bodies in
the cavity: the anterolateral and the posteromedial.

*Common mistake:* one papillary muscle is missing, or the ventricle is not round. The guideline uses this level to judge the
wall motion.

![The papillary-muscle level, found, with its checklist](../images/echo-view-psax-papillary.png)

### Parasternal short axis: apex

*Page 26.* Tilt, or slide one or two interspaces down and toward the left. The marker rule is **designed**.

You should see a small circle of left-ventricular cavity inside a thick wall. There should be no valve leaflet and, usually,
no right ventricle.

*Common mistake:* a valve leaflet is visible, so the plane is still too high. Move the probe down the ventricle.

### Apical four-chamber

*Page 26.* Put the probe at the apical impulse, near the fifth intercostal space and the mid-axillary line. The marker points
toward the bed, so the left heart is on the right of the image. The marker rule is taken from the guideline's words and the
landmarks (the plane contains the long axis and the tricuspid valve).

You should see the four chambers, the apex of the left ventricle at the top and centre, and the mitral and tricuspid leaflets.
The ventricle should taper to a point.

*Common mistake:* a round apex. The plane misses the true apex, so the ventricle looks short. This is **foreshortening**. The
hint is then "slide until it tapers to a point".

### Apical four-chamber, right-ventricle focused

*Page 26.* From the four-chamber view, rotate slightly counterclockwise, keeping the probe at the apex. Keep the plane through
the centre of the left ventricle. The turn of 20° is **designed** ("slightly" in the guideline).

You should see the right ventricle open as wide as possible, the right atrium, the tricuspid leaflets and the left ventricle.
The view is used for right-ventricular size and tricuspid annular motion.

*Common mistake:* the plane moves off the centre of the left ventricle, so the score's centre landmark leaves the plane.

### Apical five-chamber

*Page 26.* From the four-chamber view, tilt the beam anteriorly until the outflow tract of the left ventricle, the aortic valve
and the start of the ascending aorta appear. A posterior tilt shows the coronary sinus instead.

You should see the left ventricle, the aortic cusps, the aorta, the right ventricle and the right atrium. This view has the
largest hidden share of the apical views at the reference pose (about 40 % of the sector), so expect shadows.

*Common mistake:* the tilt is posterior, and the aortic valve does not appear.

### Apical two-chamber

*Pages 23 and 30.* From the four-chamber view, rotate about 60° counterclockwise.

You should see the left atrium, the mitral valve and the left ventricle, and **no right ventricle**. Only the left heart lies in
this plane.

*Common mistake:* the right ventricle is still in the image. The score penalises any right-ventricle blood in the plane.

### Apical three-chamber

*Pages 23 and 30.* From the two-chamber view, rotate a further 60° counterclockwise. The plane runs through the axis and the
aortic valve.

You should see the left atrium, the mitral valve, the left ventricle, the aortic valve and the aorta: the apical long axis.

*Common mistake:* the aortic valve and the aorta are missing, so the plane is not through the outflow tract.

### Subcostal four-chamber

*Page 31.* With the patient supine, put the transducer under the xiphoid process, with the marker toward the patient's left
(3 o'clock). Aim the beam toward the left shoulder. The marker rule follows the guideline's words.

You should see the four chambers, with the mitral and tricuspid leaflets. This view has the smallest hidden share of the
shipped views at the reference pose (none).

*Common mistake:* the marker is on the wrong side, which the page names when the marker is more than 110° from its target.

## Views this model cannot show

Two views of the guideline are **not reachable** here. They are listed on the Echo page with the reason, and their
reference poses are not drawn.

* **Subcostal inferior vena cava** (*pages 24 and 31*). The view needs a long vein of several centimetres. The pack's
  inferior vena cava is a short stub, shorter than the 30 mm the view needs (`IVC_MIN_LENGTH_MM`, a designed value).
* **Suprasternal arch** (*pages 25 and 31*). The thorax has an arch in its label volume, and the arch is found. But the best pose
  within the probe's contact limit scores 35 %, far below the 80 % that counts as found. The supine anatomy does not let the beam
  meet the plane the guideline describes. The page says so.

The patient is supine. The guideline images the parasternal and apical windows with the patient in the left lateral position,
which is not modelled.

## Hints

The hint is the one thing to change first. It is one of these:

| Hint | When |
|---|---|
| Nothing to change: hold it | The view is found. |
| The beam has left the skin's contact limit | The beam is more than 60° from the skin's normal. |
| You are over a rib: slide up or down one space | Bone lies on the beam to the target. |
| Aerated lung is in the way | Lung lies on the beam to the target. |
| The index marker points the wrong way | The marker is more than 110° from its target. |
| The plane misses the true apex, so the ventricle looks short | An apical view is foreshortened. |
| Slide, tilt, rock, angle, rotate or change the depth | The largest correctable error. The direction is given relative to the patient (toward the right shoulder, toward the left hip, and so on). |

The hint names a direction; it does not say how far. **Show me** moves the probe to the reference pose, so you can compare.

## Reference poses and hidden shares

Each view's reference pose is found by a small deterministic search from the window's starting pose. It is the pose the Show me
button uses. A pose must lie within 60 mm of its window's starting point on the skin.

The hidden share is the fraction of the heart's samples in the sector that a rib or the lung covers at the reference pose. It is
computed at rest and in expiration.

| View | Hidden share | Score at the reference pose |
|---|---:|---:|
| Parasternal long axis | 0.3 | 0.91 |
| RV outflow | 0.5 | 0.99 |
| RV inflow | 0.0 | 1.00 |
| Parasternal short axis, aortic valve | 0.2 | 1.00 |
| Parasternal short axis, mitral valve | 0.1 | 0.93 |
| Parasternal short axis, papillary | 0.1 | 0.98 |
| Parasternal short axis, apex | 0.1 | 1.00 |
| Apical four-chamber | 0.0 | 0.99 |
| Apical four-chamber, RV focused | 0.1 | 0.96 |
| Apical five-chamber | 0.4 | 0.93 |
| Apical two-chamber | 0.3 | 0.91 |
| Apical three-chamber | 0.1 | 0.99 |
| Subcostal four-chamber | 0.0 | 1.00 |

The hidden share is measured on the default anatomy pack, once. Those shares are a check on the geometry. They are not a test of
the method on patients.

## What is designed

Everything in this section is a design choice of the project. None of it has been fitted to recorded images or reviewed by a
sonographer.

**The score's tolerances.** Every tolerance lives in one struct, `ViewTolerances`, in `crates/heartsim-echo/src/views.rs`. Its
defaults are these:

| Tolerance | Default | Unit | What it does |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `plane_sigma_mm` | 8.0 | mm | Width of the penalty for a key landmark's distance from the image plane. |
| `sector_sigma_deg` | 5.0 | ° | Width of the penalty for a key landmark outside the sector's edge. |
| `sector_margin_deg` | 4.0 | ° | A key landmark counts as inside the sector only this far in from the edge. |
| `depth_sigma_mm` | 10.0 | mm | Width of the penalty for a key landmark beyond the imaging depth. |
| `depth_margin_mm` | 10.0 | mm | A key landmark must lie this far inside the imaging depth. |
| `absent_sigma_mm` | 12.0 | mm | A landmark that must be absent counts as present when this close to the plane and inside the sector. |
| `marker_sigma_deg` | 15.0 | ° | Width of the penalty for the marker's angle from its target. |
| `marker_loose_sigma_deg` | 25.0 | ° | The same, for views whose marker the guideline gives only in words. |
| `normal_sigma_deg` | 15.0 | ° | Short-axis views: width of the penalty for the plane's angle to the long axis. |
| `normal_free_deg` | 35.0 | ° | Short-axis views: no penalty up to this angle. |
| `circularity_min` | 0.85 | ratio | The left ventricle's section must be at least this round (minor to major axis). |
| `circularity_sigma` | 0.1 | ratio | Width of the penalty for a shortfall below `circularity_min`. |
| `foreshortening_sigma_mm` | 6.0 | mm | Apical views: width of the penalty for the true apex's distance from the plane. |
| `axis_sigma_deg` | 12.0 | ° | Apical views: width of the penalty for the beam's angle to the long axis. |
| `occlusion_sigma_mm` | 4.0 | mm | Window quality: width of the penalty for bone or lung along the line to a key landmark. |
| `found_threshold` | 0.8 | score | A view is found at this score or more. |
| `found_hold_s` | 1.0 | s | ... and held this long. |
| `checklist_min_fraction` | 0.0004 | share | A checklist structure is ticked at this share of the image's samples (and at least 8 samples). |
| `hint_slide_mm` | 6.0 | mm | Slide error that counts as the movement to make first. |
| `hint_rotate_deg` | 8.0 | ° | Rotation error that counts as an error. |
| `hint_tilt_deg` | 4.0 | ° | Tilt error. |
| `hint_rock_deg` | 4.0 | ° | Rock error. |
| `hint_depth_mm` | 20.0 | mm | Depth error. |
| `rib_bone_mm` | 4.0 | mm | Bone on the central line that counts as "over a rib". |
| `lung_mm` | 6.0 | mm | Lung on the central line that counts as "lung in the way". |
| `wrong_marker_deg` | 110.0 | ° | Marker error that counts as the wrong side. |
| `foreshortened_below` | 0.7 | score | The apical term below which a view is called foreshortened. |
| `seed_step_mm` | 12.0 | mm | Reference search: spacing of the starting points on the skin. |
| `max_slide_mm` | 60.0 | mm | A reference pose lies at most this far from its window's starting point. |
| `parasternal_left_margin_mm` | 15.0 | mm | The parasternal window lies at least this far to the patient's left of the midline. |

**Other designed constants** in `views.rs`, `view_hints.rs` and the Echo page:

| Constant | Value | Where and why |
|---|---|---|
| Virtual radius for planes through the axis | 25 mm | About the left ventricle's radius. |
| RV-focused four-chamber turn | 20° | "Slightly" (page 26). |
| Apical short-axis level | 1/6 of the way from apex to mitral | The middle of the apical third (page 26). |
| Minimum IVC length | 30 mm | "Several centimetres" (page 31). |
| Reference search seeds | 9 × 9 (4 rings) | Fewer seeds search faster; the shipped poses are the same with 5 rings. |
| Reference search, hidden-share weight | 3.0 | Penalises a hidden window in the search; not part of the score. |
| Reference search, second-phase slack | 0.1 | The second phase may give up about 10 % of the score for a cleaner window. |
| Reference search, minimum score | 0.91 | The second phase never goes below this score when the first phase reached it. |
| Roundness sample grid and extent | 3 mm grid, 55 mm | How the roundness of the left ventricle's section is measured. |
| Roundness minimum section | about 250 mm² | Below this the cut is a grazing or missed cut. |
| *Show me* duration | 1.4 s | Instant with Reduced motion. |
| Contact limit | 60° | From the skin's normal (the anatomy's designed value). |

The targets themselves come from the pack's landmarks: the valve centres, the apex and the long axis, the papillary bodies and
moderator band (designed), and the ribs and lungs. They are not typed by hand. A different pack gives different, still valid,
targets.

**What the score does not measure:** image quality beyond the geometric window term, the blood flow, the valve gradients, and
anything about a real patient.
